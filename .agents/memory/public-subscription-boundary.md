---
name: Public subscription delivery boundary
description: Why new subscriptions avoid public management links and do not claim email delivery
---

The public signup intentionally records interest and consent without sending mail. Do not turn a saved preference into a promise of automated alerts. New subscribers are not asked to keep a reusable link; old links remain valid only as transitional compatibility.

**Why:** The approved website feedback scope excluded campaigns and automated alert sending, and no sender was approved. The owner chose a simpler signup and email verification later rather than expecting subscribers to keep a private link. Revoking old links before verification would cut off existing unsubscribe access.

**How to apply:** Keep the no-delivery limitation visible until an approved provider and verified delivery flow exist. Use staff-assisted verified requests for later changes or unsubscribe in the meantime. An anonymous repeat signup must not reveal membership, gain control of another subscription, or reactivate a stopped subscription. Keep older links working until verified email access replaces them.

General HETV news and announcements are a separate editorial audience, not implicit consent to every role or programme notice. Opportunity preferences and an all-opportunities selection require deliberate opt-in.

**Why:** The compact email-first consent was approved on the understanding that broad news and opportunity targeting remain separate, even when a visitor skips the optional preference step.

**How to apply:** Preserve this distinction in signup copy, CRM segmentation, and matching. Verification-only email still needs a specifically approved sender; do not present email self-service as functional before then.
